How a sale works

Three beats, ten seconds

01

Punch in the price

Enter the amount in your currency on the numpad — the terminal converts to sats at the live rate. Price in dollars, or work straight in sats if that's how you run.

02

Customer scans or taps

A Lightning invoice appears as a QR for any wallet, and the built-in NFC reader takes Boltcard tap-to-pay at the same time. Whichever lands first wins.

03

Paid, printed, done

Settlement is confirmed within seconds, the PAID screen flashes up, and the thermal printer hands your customer a receipt without being asked.

Hardware

Real silicon, no black boxes

Built on the Guition JC4880P443 — an ESP32-P4 board with the display, touch and Wi-Fi already on it. Add the NFC reader and printer, print the case, and you have a terminal.

The enclosure is a 3D-printable STL in the repo, and everything wires to the board's own connectors — no custom PCB.

ProcessorESP32-P4, dual-core RISC-V @ 400 MHz
Display4.3″ IPS 480×800, MIPI-DSI, capacitive touch
Wi-FiESP32-C6 companion chip (ESP-Hosted)
Tap-to-payPN532 NFC — Boltcard / LNURL-withdraw
ReceiptsCSN-A2 thermal printer, 58 mm paper
Storage16 MB flash, dual OTA slots
SetupCaptive portal, Web Serial, or serial console
UpdatesOTA from your own host, or USB from a browser

Open source

Audit the till you run your shop on

The Bitcoin Terminal is built and released open source by Stacked — the firmware, the flasher and the enclosure all live on GitHub.

  • ✓Full firmware source — C++ on PlatformIO, one pio run from clone to flashable build
  • ✓Web flasher and firmware manifest you host on your own domain — updates never depend on us
  • ✓3D-printable enclosure included
  • ✓No telemetry, no phone-home — the device talks to your back end and a public spot-rate feed, nothing else
  • ✓MIT-licensed — swap the branding, the currency, even the payment back end; it's your code
